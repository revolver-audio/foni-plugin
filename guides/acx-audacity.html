How to pass ACX in Audacity with free tools

ACX checks three numbers on every chapter: RMS between −23 and −18 dB, peaks under −3 dB, and a noise floor under −60 dB. Peak and RMS are easy to fix with processing. The noise floor is mostly decided when you record. Here is what each number means and two free ways to pass.

The ACX specs

CheckRequirement
RMS (average level)−23 to −18 dB
Peakbelow −3 dB
Noise floorbelow −60 dB RMS
FileMP3, CBR, 192 kbps or higher, 44.1 kHz
Room tone1–5 s at start and end, never more than 5 s
Lengthmax 120 min per file, one chapter per file

Mono or stereo are both fine, as long as every file uses the same one. ACX also requires human narration unless they have authorized otherwise, opening and closing credits as separate files, and a retail sample of five minutes or less (ACX audio submission requirements).

What the three numbers mean

  • RMS is the average level of the whole chapter. Too low and listeners turn it up and hear the noise; too high and it sounds squashed.
  • Peak is the single loudest moment. Keeping it under −3 dB leaves room for MP3 encoding, which can push peaks up a little.
  • Noise floor is the level of the quietest half-second, normally the room tone between sentences. It catches computer fans, traffic, fridges and USB hum.
The catch: anything that raises the RMS also raises the noise. If your recording needs +10 dB to reach −20 dB RMS, the room tone goes up by about the same amount. A quiet room is what makes the noise floor pass, not a plugin.

Before you start

  1. Install the free ACX Check analyzer from the Audacity plugins site. It reads all three numbers for you.
  2. Export the raw recording as a WAV and keep it somewhere safe. Never edit your only copy, and never do production work on MP3s.
  3. Edit the chapter first: cuts, retakes, breaths you want gone. Process last.

Method 1: Audacity's own effects

This is the process from Audacity's Audiobook Mastering page. Select the whole chapter, then:

  1. Filter Curve EQ → Factory Presets → Low roll-off for speech. This removes rumble below the voice.
  2. Loudness Normalization → normalize RMS to −20 dB.
  3. Limiter → Soft Limit, limit to −3.50 dB.
  4. Analyze → ACX Check.

Peak and RMS should now pass. The menus above are from Audacity 3; Audacity 4 has the same effects, sometimes in different submenus. Audacity's page also warns against heavy processing: ACX's human reviewers can reject a chapter as over-processed.

Method 2: FONI's Audiobook preset

FONI is our free voice plugin (VST3, AU, CLAP and standalone, Windows and macOS). It works in Audacity like any VST3 effect (in Audacity 4: Effect → VST3 → Revolver Audio → FONI). The Audiobook (ACX-style) preset sets a gentle leveler, a −20 LUFS target, an 80 Hz low cut, a −3 dBTP ceiling and Noise cleanup. One effect does the job of steps 1–3 above, and it also evens out sentences that come out louder or quieter than the rest.

FONI aims at LUFS, not RMS, and the two differ with the track layout, so always confirm with ACX Check. We ran three voice recordings through the preset and measured them the way ACX Check does:

Mono or stereo track? Most narrators record in mono. On a mono track, keep Target at −20. If your mono voice sits on a stereo track (the same signal left and right, common in REAPER and other DAWs), LUFS counts the two channels together and the RMS ends up about 3 dB lower: set Target to −17. Not sure which you have? Switch on ACX in FONI's top bar (version 0.9.4 and later): it reads RMS, peak and noise floor live and suggests the Target if the RMS is out of range.

After export to 192 kbps MP3 the peaks stayed below −3 dB (−3.5 dB on the file we checked). The noise floor is the hard part: on the noisy recording it measured −53 dB on a mono track, above the −60 dB limit, because FONI raised the voice and the room came up with it. FONI 0.9.5 adds a real Noise cleanup that lowers this kind of steady room noise; the measurements above are from 0.9.3, so check your own result with ACX Check.

If the noise floor fails: run Audacity's Noise Reduction on the raw recording first (a light setting, sampled from your room tone), then FONI, then ACX Check. If it still fails, the fix is the room: turn off fans, move the mic closer, add soft furnishings, and record again.

Common reasons chapters fail

  • Room tone that is digital silence. Cutting pauses to pure zero can confuse ACX Check and sounds unnatural. Use real room tone.
  • More than 5 seconds of silence at the start or end of a file.
  • Normalizing peaks instead of RMS. "Normalize to −3 dB" fixes the peak and leaves the RMS wherever it was.
  • Heavy noise reduction. It passes the meter and fails the human check: watery, metallic voice.
  • Measuring the WAV and uploading a different MP3. Check the file you actually send.
